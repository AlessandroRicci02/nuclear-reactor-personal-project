# 4. ROBUST ORIFICING DESIGN

Treat the 9-ring orifice architecture as an engineering subsystem rather than merely an optimization variable.

Determine the best practical orifice configuration subject simultaneously to:

* temperature equalization;
* pressure drop;
* manufacturing tolerance;
* flow stability;
* sensitivity to power redistribution;
* off-design operation.

Do not optimize for nominal ΔT alone.

The target is a design that remains effective when:

* power distribution changes;
* total flow changes;
* pressure changes;
* geometry deviates from nominal;
* hydraulic coefficients vary.

Compare the robust discrete orificing architecture against the un-orificed baseline.

Explicitly distinguish:

* ideal mathematical flow allocation;
* reduced-order hydraulic representation;
* physically realizable discrete geometry.

Do not claim zero temperature maldistribution unless the physical model actually supports it.

* heat-transfer coefficient;
* pressure drop;
* peak cladding temperature;
* peak fuel temperature;
* outlet temperature distribution.

# 8. SECOND-STAGE ROBUST OPTIMIZATION

Only after the uncertainty model is established, perform a focused optimization.

Do not repeat V5's broad blind search.

1. minimize reactor/module mass;
2. minimize peak cladding temperature;
3. minimize pressure drop;
4. maximize lifetime;
5. minimize thermal maldistribution.

Subject to hard constraints on:

* criticality;
* shutdown margin;
* stuck-drum margin;
* negative Doppler;
* pressure drop;
* fuel/cladding temperatures.

Use robust feasibility rather than nominal feasibility wherever possible.

A candidate that passes nominally but fails under small realistic perturbations should not be considered a robust finalist.
