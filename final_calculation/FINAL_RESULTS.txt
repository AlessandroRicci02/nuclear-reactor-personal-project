10. CLADDING COATING EXPLORATION & THERMAL PENALTY ANALYSIS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question: Is putting an Al2O3 insulating layer between the fuel cladding and coolant beneficial?

Analytical Conduction Mechanics:
  - Fission heat flows OUTWARDS from the UN pellet through the Nb-1Zr clad into the bulk helium.
  - Inserting an insulating layer (Al2O3, k = 8.5 W/(m*K) dense, k = 3.0 W/(m*K) TBC) directly
    between the cladding and coolant introduces a conductive thermal barrier in series:
      Delta T_coating = q''_wall * (t_coating / k_Al2O3)
  - At the peak linear power location (q''_wall ~ 3.7e5 W/m2):
      Delta T_100um = 3.7e5 * (100e-6 / 8.5) = +4.35 K (Dense) / +12.3 K (Porous TBC)
      Delta T_250um = 3.7e5 * (250e-6 / 8.5) = +10.88 K (Dense) / +30.8 K (Porous TBC)
  - Because coolant temperature is fixed by the thermodynamic Brayton cycle, cladding temperature
    MUST RISE to overcome this additional thermal resistance.

Definitive Engineering Conclusion on Cladding Coating:
1. Cladding Temperature Degrades (Higher / Hotter):
   - Putting an insulator on the cladding forces cladding peak temperatures UP (+0.9 K to +12.3 K),
     directly degrading the Nb-1Zr thermal creep margin against the 1350 K limit.
2. Zero Moderator Protection:
   - A cladding coating does not impede hydrogen desorption from the moderator (relative loss remains 55%).
3. Neutronic Reactivity Loss:
   - Coating the cladding displaces coolant flow area or fuel volume, reducing k_eff by -239 pcm to -981 pcm.
4. Dual Layer Catastrophe:
   - Adding both 250 um clad coating and 250 um moderator liner severely depresses k_eff (-2485 pcm),
     rendering the core subcritical at BOL (k = 0.98561) while still overheating the cladding.
5. FINAL DESIGN RECOMMENDATION:
   - DO NOT place an insulator on the cladding.
   - PLACE the Al2O3 layer EXCLUSIVELY as a 100 um liner on the YH2 moderator inner boundary.


11. THERMAL-HYDRAULIC PRESSURE DROP AUDIT & PHYSICAL RESOLUTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Audit Question: Why is Core Delta P = 20.59 kPa, whereas earlier V8 reported 26.42 kPa?
Source File: final_calculation_audited/data/th_pressure_drop_audit.csv

Physical Resolution:
1. Geometry Distinction:
   - Historical Baseline Opt-17 (Cand 17-Clean) had a narrow annular gap: t_gap = 0.75 mm (0.075 cm)
     and t_clad = 0.50 mm (0.050 cm). Annular flow area per pin was 3.146e-5 m2.
   - Reference Architecture V7_CAND_BALANCED_ROBUST intentionally expanded the coolant gap to:
     t_gap = 0.84 mm (0.084 cm) and t_clad = 0.48 mm (0.048 cm).
     This expands the annular flow area per pin to 3.519e-5 m2 (+11.8% flow area expansion).
2. Frictional Scaling:
   - In narrow annular channels, frictional pressure drop scales inversely with the cube of the gap:
     Delta P_friction proportional to 1 / (t_gap^3).
   - Expanding t_gap from 0.75 mm to 0.84 mm reduces core frictional pressure drop by 34.4%
     (from 16.20 kPa down to 10.62 kPa).
3. Orifice Plate Contribution:
   - The 9-ring concentric orifice plate adds 13.09 kPa on the peak inner channel (Ring 0) and 0.0 kPa
     on the outer rings (Rings 6-8).
   - Net Core Pressure Drop = 20.59 kPa (compliant with 35.0 kPa limit with +14.41 kPa margin).
   - The values genuinely reflect different physical geometries; they are not contradictory.
